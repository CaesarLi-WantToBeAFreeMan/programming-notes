## 11 The All-time Most Costly Software Errors

* source: [11 of the most costly software errors in history](https://raygun.com/blog/costly-software-errors-history/)

### 1. The Mariner 1 Spacecraft, 1962
* `Lost`: more than **$18 million** at the time(about **$169 million** today)
* `Error`: A hyphen was omitted in a line of code, causing incorrect guidance signals to be sent to the spacecraft
* `What Happened`: NASA engineers issued a self-destruct command because the Mariner 1's rocket veered dangerously off course. The spacecraft was destroyed about 290 seconds after launch

### 2. The Morris Worm, 1988
* `Lost`: as high as **$10 million**
* `Error`: Cornell University student Robert Tappan Morris created one of the first computer worms
* `What Happened`: Robert Morris uploaded the code to an MIT server. The worm quickly spread and infected thousands of computers, significantly slowing their performance, in some cases, making them unusable

### 3. Pentium FDIV Bug, 1994
* `Lost`: upwards of **$475 million**
* `Error`: a flaw in the chip's floating-point division (`FDIV`) algorithm duo to a problem in the lookup table, which caused tiny inaccuracies in calculations, occurring roughly once every 1 in 360 billion
* `What Happened`: math professor Thomas Nicely discovered the flaw and reported it to Intel. After the issue became public, millions of users demanded replacement chips

### 4. Bitcoin Hack, Mt. Gox, 2011
* `Lost`: roughly half a billion dollars before they declared bankruptcy
* `Error`: a glitch fraudulent transactions that could never be fully redeemed
* `What Happened`: Mt. Gox, the biggest Bitcoin exchange in the early 2010s, was unable to fulfill user withdrawals. Ultimately, it declared bankruptcy

### 5. EDS Child Support System, 2004
* `Lost`: **$153 million** loss
* `Error`: an internal memo described the system as "badly designed, badly tested and badly implement", and reported that the CS2 system had over 1,000 reported problems, of which 400 had no known workaround
* `What Happened`: Electronic Data Systems was contracted the UK's Child Support Agency to develop a new system. Original budgeted at £450 million (about $597 million), the project ultimately cost around £768 million (about $1 billion)

### 6. Heathrow Terminal 5 Opening, 2008
* `Lost`: more than **£16 million** (about **$21 million**)
* `Error`: a baggage handing system that worked well during testing but failed badly during live operations
* `What Happened`: in march 2008, Heathrow's Terminal 5 experienced a major system failure. Over the next 10 days, around 42,000 bags were lose and more than 500 flights were canceled

### 7. NASA's Mars Climate Orbiter, 1998
* `Lost`: **$320 million**
* `Error`: a unit conversion mistake, imperial units were used instead of metric
* `What Happened`: In 1998, NASA launched the Mars Climate Orbiter. Duo to a failure to convert imperial measurements (used by Lockheed martin's control software) to metric units (used by NASA's onboard software), the spacecraft entered Mars' atmosphere at the wrong altitude and burned up

### 8. Soviet Gas Pipeline Explosion, 1982
* `Lost`: estimate at **$8 billion**
* `Error`: the CIA allegedly planted a bugs in the software controlling the Soviet pipeline
* `What Happened`: In June 1982, a convert CIA operation reportedly caused a massive exploration in a Soviet gas pipeline. The CIA inserted malicious code into software that was later acquired by the Soviets through a Canadian company. When activated, the software caused the pipeline's pressure to spike out of control, leading to a catastrophic exploration visible from space

### 9. Knight's $440M In Bad Trades, 2012
* `Lost`: **$440 million**
* `Error`: a software glitch caused unintended stock trades within just 30 minutes
* `What Happened`: Knight Capital Group developed a new trading software meant to boost profits. However, due to a critical bug, the software went rogue and executed a series of unintended trades, spending over $7 billion on 150 different stocks in under an hour. The resulting losses wiped out 75% of the company's value. Although Goldman Sachs helped stabilize the situation, Knight was eventually acquired by a competitor

### 10. ESA Ariane 5 Flight V88, 1996
* `Lost`: **$370 million**
* `Error`: reused incompatible code that caused a conversion error from 64-bit to 16-bit data
* `What Happened`: just 36 seconds after the maiden flight of the Ariane 5 rocket by the European Space Agency, the rocket veered off course and self-destructed due to a software failure. The software reused from Ariane 4 couldn't handle the faster horizontal velocity of Ariane 5, leading to an overflow error

### 11. The Millennium Bug, 2000
* `Lost`: estimated at **$100 billion**
* `Error`: most computers and operating systems used only two digits to represent the year, ignoring the `19` prefix for the twentieth century
* `What Happened`: the Millennium bug, also known as the infamous `Y2K`, raised global concern that systems would interpret the year 2000 as 1900, potentially causing widespread failures in everything from banking systems to air traffic control. The U.S. government and private sectors spend billions to audit and fix vulnerable code before the year 2000

## Roman Numeral

### code
```java
    import java.util.HashMap;
    import java.util.Map;

    public class RomanNumeral{
        private static final Map <Character, Integer> romanNumbers = new HashMap<>();

        static{
            romanNumbers.put('I', 1);
            romanNumbers.put('V', 5);
            romanNumbers.put('X', 10);
            romanNumbers.put('L', 50);
            romanNumbers.put('C', 100);
            romanNumbers.put('D', 500);
            romanNumbers.put('M', 1000);
        }

        private boolean isValid(String numberString){
            //empty string
            if(numberString.isEmpty())
                return false;

            //other invalid letters
            if(!numberString.matches("[IVXLCDM]+"))
                return false;

            //invalid repeats
            if(numberString.matches(".*(IIII|XXXX|CCCC|MMMM).*") ||
                numberString.matches(".*(VV|LL|DD).*"))
                return false;

            //invalid subtractive
            if(numberString.matches(".*IL|IC|ID|IM|VX|XC|VD|VM|XD|XM.*"))
                return false;
            if(numberString.matches(".*I{2,}[VX].*") ||
                numberString.matches(".*X{2,}[LC].*") ||
                numberString.matches(".*C{2,}[DM].*"))
                return false;

            return true;
        }

        public int convert(String numberString){
            if(!isValid(numberString))
                return 0;
            //roman numerals must be positive
            int result = 0, current, next;
            for(int i = 0; i < numberString.length(); i++){
                current = romanNumbers.get(numberString.charAt(i));
                next = i + 1 < numberString.length() ? romanNumbers.get(numberString.charAt(i + 1)) : 0;
                result += current >= next ? current : -current;
            }
            return result;
        }
    }
```

## Systematic Testing

### Purpose
1. find and fix bug early
2. verify the software meets requirements
3. ensure quality and reliability
4. increase user and stakeholder confidence
5. support continuous improvement
6. enable safe updates and maintenance

### Example
* look above, I systematically tested my conversion algorithm from Roman numerals to Arabic (Indian) numbers
* I tested single digit, multiple digits, subtractive notation and invalid notation scenarios
* the more cases you consider and test, the fewer bug you'll encounter

### Testing Types
1. `functional testing`
    1. `unit testing`
        * test individual parts of the program (e.g. functions, methods, etc)
    2. `integration testing`
        * test how different components or modules work together
    3. `end-to-end testing`
        * simulate real user workflows from start to finish
    4. `regression testing`
        * check that recent changes haven't broken anything that was already working
    5. `sanity testing`
        * quickly verify that a bug fix or update didn't cause other problems
    6. `smoke testing`
        * do a basic check to make sure the software is stable enough for deeper testing
    7. `user acceptance testing` (`UAT`)
        * ensure the software meets business goals and is usable for customers
    8. `interactive (manual) testing`
        * manually test features, especially useful when automation isn't available
2. `non-functional testing`
    1. `single user performance testing`
        * check how the application performs under normal conditions (no extra system load)
    2. `performance testing`
        * measure speed, stability, and resource use under specific workloads
    3. `load testing`
        * test how the system handles multiple users at the same time
    4. `stress testing`
        * push the software beyond its limits to see how it fails
    5. `security testing`
        * make sure the application is protected from threats and vulnerabilities
    6. `accessibility testing`
        * ensure the software for people with disabilities (e.g. visually impaired, hard of hearing, etc)
3. `testing approaches based on code visibility`
    1. `black-box testing`
        * test without looking at code, focus only on inputs and expected outputs
    2. `white-box testing`
        * test with access to the full recourse code, check logic, flow and internal behavior

## JUnit

### Introduction
* `JUnit` is a test automation framework for the Java programming language
* `JUnit` is often used for unit testing, and is one of the `xUnit` framework
* `JUnit` is linked as a `JAR` at compile-time:
    1. package `org.junit.jupiter` for `JUnit5`
    2. package `org.junit` for `JUnit4`
    3. package `junit.framework` for `JUnit3`

### Lifecycle
1. Setup Phrase
    * preparation step before tests run
    1. `@BeforeAll`
        * library: `org.junit.jupiter.api.BeforeAll`
        * runs once before all tests
        * used for things that take time to set up (like connect to a database)
    2. `@BeforeEach`
        * library: `org.junit.jupiter.api.BeforeEach`
        * runs before each test
        * set up a fresh environment for every single thing
2. Test Execution Phrase
    1. `@Test`
        * library: `org.junit.jupiter.api.Test`
        * marks the code that runs the test and checks the result
3. Cleanup Phrase
    1. `@AfterAll`
        * library: `org.junit.jupiter.api.AfterAll`
        * runs once after all tests finish
        * used to close things like database connections
    2. `@AfterEach`
        * library: `org.junit.jupiter.api.AfterEach`
        * runs after each test
        * resets things so the next test starts fresh

## Output Test Result to a Long File Using JUnit Lifecycle

### explanation
1. create a `logs` directory if it doesn't already exist
2. generate a file named using the format `yyyy-MM-dd_HH-mm-ss_(z)_log.txt` e.g. `2025-04-17_19-03-51_(CST)_log.txt`
3. for each test, write its detailed result to the log file in the following format
    ```
        Running Test: ?
        test string:	?
        expected value:	?
        actual value:	?
        result:	PASS or FAIL
        runtime:	? ms

        ------------
    ```
4. after all tests finish, write a summary like this    
    ```
        ============ summary ============
        passed:	?
        failed:	?
        runtime:	? ms
    ```
5. finally, close the file

### code
```java
    import java.io.BufferedWriter;
    import java.io.IOException;

    import java.nio.file.Files;
    import java.nio.file.Path;
    import java.nio.file.Paths;

    import java.time.ZoneId;
    import java.time.ZonedDateTime;
    import java.time.format.DateTimeFormatter;

    import org.junit.jupiter.api.BeforeAll;
    import org.junit.jupiter.api.BeforeEach;
    import org.junit.jupiter.api.Test;
    import org.junit.jupiter.api.AfterEach;
    import org.junit.jupiter.api.AfterAll;
    import org.junit.jupiter.api.TestInfo;

    public class RomanNumeralTest{
        private static BufferedWriter logWriter;
        private static int passed = 0, failed = 0;
        private static long begin;

        //create a log file
        @BeforeAll
        static public void setupLogFile() throws IOException{
            begin = System.nanoTime();//mark the starting time for total runtime
            Path directory = Paths.get("logs");
            if(!Files.exists(directory))
                Files.createDirectory(directory);//make "logs" folder if it doesn't exist
            ZonedDateTime now = ZonedDateTime.now(ZoneId.of("Asia/Taipei"));//specify time zone to Taipei
            String formattedDate = now.format(DateTimeFormatter.ofPattern("yyyy-MM-dd_HH-mm-ss_(z)"));
            Path logFile = directory.resolve(formattedDate + "_log.txt");
            logWriter = Files.newBufferedWriter(logFile);
        }

        //log the name of the test
        @BeforeEach
        void beforeEach(TestInfo testInfo) throws IOException {
            logWriter.write("Running Test: " + testInfo.getDisplayName() + "\n");
        }

        //a test simple
        @Test
        public void singleDigit1() throws IOException{
            writeResult("I", 1, result, System.nanoTime());
        }

        //tests

        //log test details, check result, update counters
        private void writeResult(String testString, int expectedValue, int actualValue, long start) throws IOException{
            if(actualValue == expectedValue)
                passed++;
            else
                failed++;

            logWriter.write("test string:\t" + testString + "\n" +
                            "expected value:\t" + expectedValue + "\n" +
                            "actual value:\t" + actualValue + "\n" +
                            "result:\t" + (actualValue == expectedValue ? "PASS" : "FAIL") + "\n" +
                            "runtime:\t" + ((System.nanoTime() - start) / 1_000_000) + " ms");
        }

        //add a separator for readability
        @AfterEach
        public void after() throws IOException{
            logWriter.write("\n\n------------\n\n");
        }

        //write a summary and close the file
        @AfterAll
        public static void close() throws IOException{
            logWriter.write("============ summary ============\n" +
                            "passed:\t" + passed + "\n" +
                            "failed:\t" + failed + "\n" +
                            "runtime:\t" + ((System.nanoTime() - begin) / 1_000_000) + " ms\n");
            logWriter.close();
        }
    }
```

## Terms

1. `failure`
    * a component or system behaves in a way that it's not expected
2. `flaw`
    * in a component, it can cause that system to behave incorrectly
3. `defect`
    * if encountered during execution, may cause a failure
4. `error`
    * a human action that produces an incorrect result
5. `validation`
    * are we building the right system
    * focus on **user requirements**
6. `verification`
    * are we building the system right
    * focus on **logics**

## Enough Testing

* quoted by famous Netherlands computer scientist Edsger Dijkstra
    > Testing shows the presence of defects

    > Testing does not show the absence of defects
* but there's no way to give us the assurance that the software is 100% bug-free

### `pesticide paradox`
> Every method you use to prevent or find bugs leaves a residue of subtler bugs against which those methods are ineffectual
* we need variation

## Exploratory Testing

* A famous introduction
    > No matter how many test cases of how many types you’ve created, you will run out of formally planned tests. You can keep testing.  Run new tests as you think of them, without spending much time preparing or explaining the tests. Trust your instincts
* unlike traditional testing that follows pre-written test cases, exploratory testing involves simultaneous learing, test design, and execution
